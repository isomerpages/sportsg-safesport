<h4>Safe Sport is not a box to be ticked, it is a culture. </h4>
<p>Culture is shaped in rooms like the one we gathered in recently, where
coaches, high performance managers, and practitioners from across Singapore’s <strong>National Sports Associations</strong> came
together to ask the hard questions that matter most.</p>
<div class="isomer-image-wrapper">
<img style="width: 100%" height="auto" width="100%" alt="A group workshop in a conference room with seating in small circles." src="/images/NSA_Workshop_2.png">
</div>
<p>Our&nbsp;latest <strong>Safe Sport Workshop for NSA Personnel Working with Athletes</strong> brought
together 50 participants to examine why safeguarding matters, what risk
factors look like within their own environments, and how they can more
intentionally support the athletes in their care.</p>
<p>Using the socioecological model, we explored how organisational culture
and unspoken norms can quietly enable harmful behaviours to persist. Two
key risk factors consistently surfaced across discussions: fear of speaking
up due to perceived consequences, and cultures of tolerance that normalise
concerning behaviour.</p>
<div class="isomer-image-wrapper">
<img style="width: 100%" height="auto" width="100%" alt="A group of five people stand engaged around a flipchart in a spacious room with scattered chairs" src="/images/NSA_Workshop_3.png">
</div>
<p>These sentiments reflect broader evidence in sport. Research by Hurst,
Kavussanu &amp; Stirling (2025) highlights that elite athletes report lower
perceptions of psychological safety compared to recreational athletes,
making them less likely to disclose experiences of interpersonal violence.
This gap is not just theoretical, it is lived, and it is one the sport
system must collectively address.</p>
<p>A key shift in thinking emerged during the workshop:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“The reassurance that Safe Sport is not about training weak athletes."</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Through the challenge–support model by Fletcher and Sarkar, participants
examined how high performance and athlete wellbeing are not opposing forces.
Athletes can be pushed to excel while still being supported in environments
that prioritise trust, respect, and psychological safety.</p>
<div class="isomer-image-wrapper">
<img style="width: 100%" height="auto" width="100%" alt="Group photo of diverse individuals in a spacious room, smiling widely" src="/images/NSA_Workshop_1.png">
</div>
<p>Participants reflected deeply on their own roles in shaping these supportive
environments and what it would take to move closer to a truly facilitative
culture.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“Thinking more intentionally about where my programme fits in the model.”</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>“Our responsibility in creating and building a safe environment for all.”</p>
</blockquote>
<p>What stood out most was not just awareness but ownership, a shared recognition
that Safe Sport is built through everyday decisions, behaviours, and the
norms we choose to uphold.</p>
